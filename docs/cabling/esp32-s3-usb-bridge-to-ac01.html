AC01 Bridge Cable

Programming, console and reset control for the IRMACOS-AC-01 from the ESP32-S3-USB-Bridge, with the AC01 powered from the bridge. Six wires for the PROG lane; four more if JTAG is wanted.

Both boards are 3.3 V logic — no level shifting. The bridge drives BOOT and RST open-drain with internal pull-ups (upstream commit 7534bb5), so it never fights the AC01’s own RST button or its pull-up/100 nF network; it simply pulls low when esptool asks.

Power — feeding the AC01 from the bridge

The AC01 is a 24 V board: its 3-pin PSU module drops +24 V to the single VCC rail. On the schematic the WROOM’s 3V3 pin (U1 pin 2), the pull-ups and the JTAG/PROG VCC pins all hang on that same VCC supply symbol — there is no other regulator — so VCC is the WROOM’s 3.3 V rail. Wire 4 back-feeds it from the bridge’s LDO.

Default · what the cable above does

3V3 from U2 pin 5 → PROG pin 4

Works for flashing and console. The budget is the catch: the bridge’s LDO is an unmarked SOT-223 AMS1117-class part (10 µF in/out, no heatsinking copper) already feeding its own ESP32-S3. Realistic spare is ~200–300 mA. A WROOM transmitting on Wi-Fi peaks at ~500 mA — expect brown-outs or LDO thermal throttling once the AC01 firmware brings Wi-Fi up.

Alternative · verify the PSU module first

5 V from U2 pin 4 → AC01 +24V terminal

U2 pin 4 is USB VBUS straight through (TVS only, no fuse). If the AC01’s PSU module is a wide-input buck (TSR-1 / K78xx class, Vin min ≈ 4.5 V), 5 V into the +24V terminal gives a properly regulated 3.3 V from the AC01’s own converter, with the powered hub supplying the current. Relays stay dead (24 V coils) — fine for programming. Read the module’s part number before trying this.

Never both. With wire 4 connected, leave the AC01’s 24 V supply off. The bridge’s LDO output has no reverse-blocking diode (verified on the schematic), so two regulators would fight on the same rail. If the AC01 will be on its own 24 V supply during a session, pull wire 4 out of the housing — a 5-wire cable is the safe variant for a powered target.

GPIO12 strapping. On the WROOM, MTDI/GPIO12 also selects flash voltage at reset: high → 1.8 V → the AC01 fails to boot. The bridge firmware forces TDI low across a target reset for ESP32 targets (debug_probe_handle_esp32_tdi_bootstrapping), so a connected JTAG cable is safe through esptool resets — but pull it if you see boot failures with JTAG plugged and nothing else changed.

Before plugging in — three meter checks

  1. Bridge pin numbering. USB in, cable off: U2 pin 4 reads 5 V to pin 1 and pin 5 reads 3.3 V. If 5 V shows up on a different pin, the header is numbered the other way — re-map before crimping.
  2. AC01 rail and pin 1. 24 V applied, cable off: PROG pin 6 (VCC) to pin 1 (GND) reads 3.3 V. This doubles as the orientation check — if 3.3 V appears between the other two end pins, you are counting from the wrong end.
  3. Continuity on the finished cable. PROG 1 ↔ bridge 6, PROG 2 ↔ bridge 3, PROG 3 ↔ bridge 2, PROG 4 ↔ bridge 7, PROG 5 ↔ bridge 8, PROG 6 ↔ bridge 5. Nothing else should beep.

First power-up: 24 V off, cable in, USB in. The AC01’s LED1/LED2 come alive from the back-fed rail; the bridge’s CDC port then shows the AC01 console, and esptool chip_id through the bridge proves BOOT/RST.
