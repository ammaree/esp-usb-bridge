Host Target Cabling

Programming and console cables between the three bridge host boards and the three IRMACOS target boards. Every pin below is read off the board's own schematic; nothing is inferred from a photo or a connector's silkscreen.

Read the labels carefully — the two ends use opposite conventions

Host headers name signals from the host's point of view: TX is the host driving the target. Target headers name them from the target's point of view: TX is the target's own output. So a cable always joins host TX to target RX and host RX to target TX — the labels read as a crossover even though the function is a straight link.

The tables below are laid out by signal rather than as nine separate cables: read the host pin from one column and the target pin from another, and any of the nine combinations falls out directly.

Host boards

ESP32-S3-USB-Bridge U2 — 2×6 right-angle, both lanes on one connector 3V3 is a small LDO shared with the bridge's own ESP32-S3. Pin 4 is USB VBUS straight through (TVS only, no fuse).
MuseLab ESPLink v1.2 J3 serial/control and J4 JTAG — two 1×6 headers JTAG lines carry series resistors from pack RP1. J3 offers 3V3, J4 offers 5V.
ESP-Prog-2 PROG 6-pin 2.54 mm and JTAG 10-pin 1.27 mm VDD on both headers is jumper-selectable 3.3 V or 5 V (PROG PWR SEL / JTAG PWR SEL). JTAG pin 10 is NC.

Target boards

IRMACOS-AC-01 v1.4b PROG 1×6  ·  JTAG 1×6
IRMACOS-RS-01 v1.7d PRG 2×5 (adds I²C)  ·  JTAG 1×6 Pins 7–10 are I²C (SDA/SCL/IO18/IO19), not part of a programming cable.
IRMACOS-RS-02 v2.4 PRG 2×3  ·  JTG 2×5

JTAG lane — four wires plus a ground

Optional, and only for debugging. On every one of these targets JTAG lands on the ESP32's MTDI/MTCK/MTMS/MTDO = GPIO12/13/14/15. Leave the target's JTAG VCC pin unconnected — power comes over the serial lane.

GPIO12 decides flash voltage at reset. On the ESP32 it must be low as EN rises, or the chip selects 1.8 V flash and fails to boot. The bridge firmware forces TDI low across a target reset, so a connected JTAG cable is safe through esptool resets — but if a target stops booting with JTAG plugged in and nothing else changed, unplug the JTAG lane first.

Power

  • Never feed the target from two sources. With the 3V3 wire fitted, leave the target's own 24 V supply off. None of these hosts has a reverse-blocking diode on its 3V3 output.
  • ESP32-S3-USB-Bridge shares one small LDO with its own ESP32-S3; only a couple of hundred milliamps are spare. A WROOM target peaks well above that on boot, and an AC01 fed this way was seen to log flash read err and retry before coming up. Its U2 pin 4 is USB VBUS straight through, so feeding 5 V into the target's own regulator is the better option where the target accepts it.
  • ESPLink offers 3V3 on J3 pin 1 and 5 V on J4 pin 6.
  • ESP-Prog-2 has jumper-selected VDD on each header — check PROG PWR SEL is on 3.3 V before connecting to a 3.3 V rail.
  • All three targets are 24 V boards whose on-board PSU module produces the single VCC rail that feeds the ESP32 directly; on these boards VCC is 3.3 V.

Before first power-up

  • Meter the host's power pin against its ground pin and confirm the voltage before the cable touches the target.
  • Meter the target's VCC to GND on its own 24 V supply and confirm 3.3 V. This doubles as an orientation check — if the voltage shows up between the wrong pair, you are counting from the wrong end of the header.
  • Ring the finished cable through against the table above; nothing outside those pairs should beep.
  • Closing the serial port leaves the target held in reset, because the host parks the lines in the state that means "reset". Attach with idf.py monitor --no-reset, or esptool --before no-reset, to avoid disturbing a running target.
