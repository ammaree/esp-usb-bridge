Bridge LED Signalling

What each LED means on each supported host board. Boards with an addressable RGB LED run a priority-ordered status scheme; boards with discrete LEDs keep the simpler activity-only behaviour.

Two schemes, chosen by the board's hardware

The firmware drives up to three discrete GPIO LEDs (CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_LED1..3) and optionally one addressable WS2812 (CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_RGB_LED, default -1 = none). Any of them may be set to -1 where the board has no such LED.

  • Discrete LEDs show activity only, one LED per role: serial TX, serial RX, debug probe. This is upstream's behaviour and is unchanged.
  • The RGB LED shows a single state at a time, chosen by priority, because one light cannot usefully mix three meanings. It is the only indicator on boards whose activity LEDs are wired to the UART lines in hardware.

RGB status scheme

Highest priority wins; the LED is dark when nothing is happening. Brightness is deliberately low — this is a status light, not illumination.

PriorityAppearanceStateMeaning
1 red, fast blink (10 Hz) fatal error eub_abort() — the bridge has failed and is about to restart. Lasts ~3.5 s.
2 red, slow pulse (1 Hz) target held in reset RST is being driven low. Normal for a moment during a reset; if it stays, the host has parked the serial lines in the reset state — typically a closed terminal. The target is not dead, it is being held.
3 amber, solid target strapped for download BOOT is low. The target is being put into, or held in, ROM download mode.
4 magenta, pulse (2 Hz) flashing An MSC/UF2 flash session owns the transport and is writing the target.
5 blue, flash debug probe activity JTAG or SWD traffic.
6 green, flash serial activity UART traffic in either direction. Direction is not distinguished here — see each board below.
7 off idle Nothing in progress. Also dark when no host is attached.

Activity pulses are stretched to a minimum of 100 ms. Without that they would be a few microseconds long and invisible — the firmware raises and drops the activity flag either side of a single UART write. The LED is refreshed only when the colour actually changes, so an idle bridge sends no WS2812 frames at all.

Red fast versus red slow is the distinction that matters. A fast blink is the bridge failing; a slow pulse is the bridge deliberately holding your target in reset. The second is far more common and is usually the answer to "why is my target dead".

LED1/LED2/LED3 are all -1: GPIO 35/36/37 are unconnected on this board, and the TX/RX indication is already done in hardware. The RGB LED therefore does not duplicate the per-direction split — the two hardwired LEDs give you that for free, and the RGB is spent on state you cannot otherwise see.

Verified on hardware 2026-09-01. Green (serial), the slow red pulse (target held in reset) and solid amber (BOOT asserted) were each driven in turn and were clearly visible, correct in colour, and comfortable in brightness at the default level. The WS2812 takes its VDD from the 5 V rail while the ESP32-S3 drives DIN at 3.3 V; that is marginal on paper but works on this board, so it needs no level shifter.

Series resistors are 10 k, so these run at roughly 0.15 mA — expect them dim. There is no power LED. The firmware's TX/RX callback swap is deliberate and matches MuseLab's own net names (TARGET_LED_TX lights when the target transmits); do not "correct" it.

D1 may not be fitted. The red LED is absent from the v1.2 schematic's BOM, so on some boards GPIO12 drives nothing and the practical inventory is green + blue. Check yours before treating a dark D1 as a fault.

None of the RGB states apply here. A fatal error shows as the three LEDs blinking a fixed seven-step pattern over ~3.5 s.

Espressif document a single indicator that shows red for the 3.3 V rail, green while downloading or passing serial, and blue during JTAG debugging — i.e. one multi-colour package rather than three separate lamps. It is not addressable, so CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_RGB_LED stays -1 and the RGB scheme does not run.

Unverified, and one oddity. These GPIO numbers are upstream's and have not been checked against ESP-Prog-2 hardware here. LED2 and LED3 are both assigned GPIO 34, so serial-from-target and JTAG activity drive the same lamp and cannot be told apart. That may be intentional for a two-colour part, or may be a defect carried in the default config — confirm against a board before relying on it.

Adding a board

  • Set CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_LED1..3 to the board's activity LEDs, or -1 where there is none. CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_LEDn_ACTIVE selects the polarity.
  • Set CONFIG_BRIDGE_GPIO_RGB_LED to a WS2812 data pin to enable the status scheme, or leave it -1.
  • All four may be -1; the firmware then drives nothing and skips the GPIO and RMT setup entirely.
  • Do not point an activity LED at a pin that carries the UART itself. Where a board wires its indicators to the UART lines, those LEDs are hardware and must be left as -1.
